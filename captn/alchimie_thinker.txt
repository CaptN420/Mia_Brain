The 10 Most Famous Alchemists (and Early Chemists):
1. Hermes Trismegistus - Legendary Hellenistic figure, "As above so below," founder of Hermeticism, attributed the Emerald Tablet
2. Jabir ibn Hayyan (Geber) - 8th-century Persian polymath, distillation techniques, arsenic isolation, "father of chemistry," described sulfur/mercury theory
3. Paracelsus (Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim) - 16th-century Swiss physician, introduced chemical medicine, laudanum, alkhemia terminology
4. Roger Bacon - 13th-century English Franciscan, experimental science emphasis, gunpowder recipes, optical lens experiments
5. John Dee - 16th-century English mathematician/astrologer, Enochian system, navigation, alchemical manuscripts collection
6. Basil Valentine - Legendary 15th-century monk, antimony work, distillation processes, monastery alchemy traditions
7. Fulcanelli - 20th-century mysterious French alchemist, "The Mysterious Stranger" commentary, medieval transmutation lore
8. Albertus Magnus - 13th-century German bishop/scholar, Thomas Aquinas teacher, metal transmutation attempts, natural philosophy
9. Cornelius Agrippa - 16th-century German occultist, Three Books of Occult Philosophy, kabbalah, ceremonial magic
10. Basilius Valentinus - 15th-century German monk, Monkshood (Aconite) distillation, early chemical experimentation

Alchemy-to-Chemistry Transition: These figures bridged mystical and practical traditions. The gradual shift from qualitative alchemical speculation to quantitative chemical analysis (Lavoisier, Boyle) established modern chemistry, while many alchemical apparatus and techniques (distillation, sublimation, calcination) remain in use today.
